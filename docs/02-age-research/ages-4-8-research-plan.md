# Ages 4–8 India Deep Research Plan

**Milestone:** Research Foundation v0.2  
**Status:** Next active research phase

## Why split 4–8 further

A four-year-old and an eight-year-old differ substantially in reading independence, attention, school expectations, motor skills, device use, parent involvement, and capacity for abstract learning. Research should therefore examine at least two subgroups:

- **4–6:** early childhood / foundational stage
- **7–8:** early primary / increasing academic independence

## Core research questions

### Daily life
- What does a normal weekday look like?
- School duration?
- Commute?
- Homework duration?
- Tuition?
- Free play?
- Family reading?
- TV/YouTube/mobile use?
- Sleep?

### Learning problems
- Which skills generate the most parent intervention?
- Where do children commonly get stuck?
- Are problems conceptual, language-related, attention-related, practice-related, or school-process-related?
- How do families currently solve them?

### Language
- Home language?
- School medium?
- Preferred explanation language?
- English-speaking aspiration?
- Does the child naturally code-switch?
- Which concepts become harder when taught only in school language?

### Parent involvement
- How much time is spent supervising?
- What work do parents perform: explain, read instructions, search YouTube, check answers, motivate, communicate with teachers?
- What do parents wish could happen without them?
- What do they refuse to delegate to software?

### Tuition and spending
- Is tuition used at this age?
- For which subjects?
- Why was it started?
- What alternatives were tried first?
- What is the real willingness to pay for software?

### Device behavior
- Dedicated or shared device?
- Typical session length?
- Child navigates independently?
- Voice use?
- Typing ability?
- Camera use?
- Parent PIN/profile switching?
- Offline need?

### Motivation
- What activities are voluntarily repeated?
- What causes abandonment?
- How do children react to correction?
- Which rewards help and which distract?
- Does narrative/context increase persistence?

### Reading and numeracy
- What are realistic foundational capabilities by age?
- What variation exists inside each nominal grade?
- Which prerequisite chains matter most?
- How should mastery be assessed without making the experience feel like testing?

### Creativity and curiosity
- What questions do children spontaneously ask?
- Drawing, storytelling, pretend play, building, singing, movement?
- Can academic concepts attach naturally to these behaviors?

### Physical-world learning
- What can safely be done away from screen?
- What needs adult supervision?
- What household materials are commonly available?
- Can the app verify completion without invasive camera/data collection?

### Safety
- What parent controls are expected?
- What content should never be generated freely?
- How should voice conversations be retained or not retained?
- How should AI respond to personal/emotional disclosures?
- How do we prevent emotional dependency?

## Research outputs required for v0.2

1. age-specific development/interaction matrix;
2. daily-life journey map;
3. parent jobs-to-be-done;
4. child jobs-to-be-done;
5. existing-solution map;
6. screen-time/attention constraints;
7. language model for India;
8. foundational reading/numeracy problem tree;
9. safety requirements;
10. opportunity hypotheses ranked by severity and evidence;
11. explicit list of ideas rejected after research;
12. recommendation on whether 4–8 should be the first product wedge.

## Definition of done

Research v0.2 is not complete merely because we have many sources. It is complete when we can explain:

- what important problem we believe exists;
- for exactly whom;
- in what context;
- how they solve it today;
- why current solutions are insufficient;
- what evidence supports that belief;
- what evidence contradicts it;
- and what still needs direct interviews or prototype testing.
