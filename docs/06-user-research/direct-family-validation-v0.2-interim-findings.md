# Direct Family Validation v0.2 — Interim Findings

**Status:** Interim qualitative field evidence; not a final validation verdict  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Prototype:** Validation v0.2 / v0.2.1  

## Evidence boundary

These notes preserve only anonymized aggregate patterns reported from direct parent/child use. No participant names, individual reviews, recordings, or identifying details are stored in this public repository.

The current evidence is qualitative. No percentages should be inferred because an exact sample size and per-session structured records are not available here.

## Observed patterns

1. **Most Grade 1 children used their fingers to solve the subtraction tasks.**
   - Treat this as a strategy signal, not failure.
   - The product should model support strategy separately from answer correctness.
   - A child can be correct while still relying on concrete/finger support.
   - Future mastery evidence should distinguish supported/concrete success from increasingly fluent independent success without shaming the strategy.

2. **The Grade 2 starting question took additional time for some children.**
   - This is not automatically evidence that the question is too difficult.
   - The current purpose of the starting probe is to surface strategy and prerequisite gaps, not maximize speed.
   - Response time should remain contextual evidence only and must not become a score.
   - Continue observing whether children eventually solve independently, request hints, or need the prerequisite step-back.

3. **Hint 1 was usually sufficient for most children.**
   - This is a promising signal for graduated scaffolding.
   - The system should always try the least-intrusive useful hint before revealing a more concrete representation.
   - Hint 2 should remain available for children who need stronger support rather than being shown automatically.
   - This supports the principle of fading assistance rather than immediately giving the answer.

4. **Parents liked and understood the parent report.**
   - Early evidence supports plain-language reporting over XP, streaks, or generic scores.
   - Parents appear able to understand strategy, support level, and independent-performance framing.
   - This remains qualitative evidence; future rounds should test whether the report changes a parent decision or makes them want to reuse the product.

## Current interpretation

These observations do **not** falsify the leading product thesis. They modestly strengthen four parts of it:

- the product should observe *how* a child solves, not only whether the answer is correct;
- grade can set the starting point while actual performance determines the next step;
- graduated hints are more appropriate than immediately revealing an explanation;
- parent-facing evidence of learning appears understandable and potentially valuable.

The strongest unresolved question is still **independent transfer**: after hints or teaching, can the child solve a new problem without assistance, and can that learning persist into a later session?

## Do not change yet

Do not redesign the grade paths simply because some children take time or use fingers. Those behaviours are currently useful diagnostic signals. Avoid optimizing for speed.

Do not remove Hint 2 simply because Hint 1 works for many children. A minority who need deeper scaffolding are important to the product thesis.

## Next field observations required

For the next family sessions, prioritize these aggregate patterns:

1. How often does the child solve the **final independent-transfer problem without any hint or adult help**?
2. For Grade 1 children who begin with finger counting, do they still need fingers on the final problem, or does support naturally reduce?
3. For Grade 2 children who take longer, do they eventually succeed independently, use Hint 1, need Hint 2, or require the prerequisite step-back?
4. When Hint 1 works, what exactly seems to unlock the child: wording, decomposition, counting cue, or visual representation?
5. Does the parent report merely make sense, or does it make the parent say they would use the product again in a real after-school situation?
6. Can the child solve a similar problem in a later session/day without the same level of support?

## Interim product rule reinforced

> **Correctness tells us what happened. Strategy, support, transfer, and retention tell us what the child actually knows.**

## Verdict at this stage

**Continue validation without a major product pivot.**

The current evidence is encouraging but insufficient to lock the product thesis, architecture, or MVP. Continue collecting aggregate direct-family observations until independent transfer, retention, reuse intent, and parent-value signals are clearer.
