# Kids Learning OS — Recruitment & Interview Kit v1.0

**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Purpose:** Execute the first direct-validation round consistently and ethically.

---

# 1. Who to recruit first

Target parents/caregivers of children aged 6–8, preferably Grades 1–2.

Seek variation across:

- CBSE / State Board / ICSE where accessible;
- no tuition / occasional tuition / regular tuition;
- English-medium / regional-medium;
- Telugu/Hindi/other multilingual households;
- metro / Tier 2 / Tier 3;
- shared-phone / dedicated-device households;
- working-parent and at-home-caregiver contexts.

Do not recruit only highly technical or EdTech-heavy parents.

---

# 2. First-round target

Aim for 20–30 parent interviews.

A useful first batch could be:

- 8–10 families with no regular tuition;
- 8–10 families with some/regular tuition;
- 4–10 families from contrasting school/language/device contexts.

This is qualitative discovery, not a statistically representative survey.

---

# 3. Recruitment message

Suggested wording:

> I’m researching how parents of Grade 1–2 children handle homework and learning when their child gets stuck. This is not a sales call and I’m not asking you to buy anything. I’d like to understand what actually happens at home today — homework, tuition, learning apps, languages, and what is frustrating. The conversation should take around 20–30 minutes. No child needs to participate unless you separately choose to join a later prototype test.

Avoid saying:

> “We are building an amazing AI tutor. Do you think this is useful?”

That biases the interview.

---

# 4. Consent and privacy

For parent interview:

- explain the research purpose;
- ask before recording audio/video;
- note that recording is optional;
- avoid collecting unnecessary personal identifiers;
- do not request school account credentials, child IDs, report cards, medical information, or sensitive family data unless a later study has a clear need and stronger consent process.

For child testing:

- explicit parent/guardian consent;
- parent present or immediately available;
- short, age-appropriate sessions;
- child may stop at any point;
- no framing as intelligence/ability testing;
- no clinical or developmental diagnosis.

---

# 5. Interview structure

### 0–5 min — routine

Understand school day, after-school routine, device access, homework responsibility.

### 5–15 min — last real problem

Deeply reconstruct the most recent time the child got stuck.

Focus on actions:

- what happened;
- what the child did;
- what the parent did;
- how long it took;
- which tools/services were used;
- whether the child ultimately understood.

### 15–20 min — alternatives and spending

Explore tuition, YouTube, worksheets, apps, school support and existing paid products.

### 20–25 min — language / trust / AI

Understand home language, school medium, microphone/camera concerns and AI familiarity.

### Last 5 min — concept reaction

Only now explain the proposed learning-bridge idea briefly and ask what feels useful, unrealistic or concerning.

---

# 6. Interviewer rules

Do:

- ask “Tell me about the last time…”;
- ask “What happened next?”;
- tolerate silence;
- follow surprising behavior;
- capture exact language;
- ask what they currently pay/do;
- distinguish what they say from what they actually do.

Do not:

- teach them the answer you want;
- defend the idea;
- turn objections into debates;
- ask leading questions;
- count compliments as evidence;
- make safety/learning claims we have not proven.

---

# 7. Best evidence hierarchy

Strong:

1. repeated recent behavior;
2. actual money/time spent;
3. current workaround;
4. recurring frustration;
5. participant asks to use prototype again;
6. parent changes behavior after trial;
7. actual willingness to pay after value is experienced.

Weak:

- “Sounds nice.”
- “I would definitely use that.”
- “AI is the future.”
- “Children need personalized education.”

---

# 8. Post-interview synthesis

Immediately record:

- strongest pain;
- most recent concrete example;
- current workaround;
- frequency;
- cost/time burden;
- why current solution is insufficient;
- trust concern;
- language insight;
- evidence for/against P1/P2/P3;
- candidate prototype test;
- surprising contradiction.

Then score confidence:

- Strong support;
- Moderate support;
- Neutral;
- Moderate contradiction;
- Strong contradiction.

---

# 9. Signals worth clustering

After every 5 interviews, cluster findings around:

- homework friction;
- parent time burden;
- tuition dependence;
- concept-gap visibility;
- child frustration;
- English/home-language tension;
- device constraints;
- app/YouTube use;
- trust/privacy;
- willingness to pay;
- independent learning;
- school/teacher communication.

Do not wait until interview 30 to synthesize.

---

# 10. Decision after round one

At the end of the first parent round, choose one:

### Continue P1 strongly
Evidence supports after-school learning bridge.

### Modify P1
Problem is real but trigger/value proposition differs.

### Promote P2
Daily foundation coach has stronger pull than homework rescue.

### Promote P3
Parent visibility/diagnosis is the dominant need.

### Change age/segment
Another segment shows materially stronger pain.

### Pause/pivot thesis
Current problem is not strong enough.

No outcome is considered a research failure.
